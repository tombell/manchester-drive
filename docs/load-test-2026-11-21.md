# Manchester Drive load test

21 November 2026. One Fly Machine in London ran the game on one shared vCPU with 256 MiB RAM. A separate London Machine with two performance vCPUs and 4 GiB RAM ran the clients over the private network. The game used Bun 1.4.4, 25 NPC cars and 39 NPC pedestrians.

Clients started close to Piccadilly Gardens for the crowd layout and at eight locations for the spread layout. Each client opened an event stream and sent sequenced input roughly every 800 ms. One in four tried to park and walk. One in eight panned the free camera. The runner started measuring six seconds after the last batch joined.

## What the runs showed

- With 64 players across eight locations the filtered runs averaged 3.95 and 3.81 KiB per delivered snapshot. The previous format averaged 21.48 KiB. At Piccadilly more players could see one another. Its filtered runs averaged 20.03 and 20.43 KiB against 23.54 KiB previously.
- The first 64-player runs met the 60 Hz simulation and 15 Hz delivery targets. After sustained load the repeats delivered 8.8 snapshots per second per client at Piccadilly and 9.9 across eight locations. Linux guest CPU counters recorded 85% and 81% steal time respectively over those runs, including setup and cleanup. Steal time is time the guest was ready to run but the host did not schedule it.
- After the 64-player repeats, a final 32-player Piccadilly run delivered 13.8 snapshots per client per second with 0 stream failures. Its simulation ran at 55.4 Hz, and its input p95 was 244 ms.
- At 128 players the Piccadilly crowd lost 12 event streams and one player session over two minutes. Across eight locations all 128 streams stayed connected, but the simulation ran at 22.9 Hz and inputs took 880 ms at the 95th percentile.

One previous-format Piccadilly attempt lost a stream before measurement and was retried. The table includes the completed retry.
